# Synthesis

**Legibility:** partly. The runs that played deep read the world as intent.md has it. Explorer-1-opus named the trials as restraint and giving things up, the double under the hood, the thief with borrowed eyes, the lake that takes everything, and an examiner who keeps reviving you. Goal-seeker-1-opus turned the old man in the cell into a shopping list of the seven things. The newcomer got the core of Q1: you fight yourself and unhood it. The four short or stuck runs got only the frame: a Zork-like empire, a dream, an old man's final test, a hooded swordsman. Neither sonnet run that killed the figure understood the mercy until afterwards. No run said anything about Q3's changed past beyond explorer-1-opus's 777 plaque. One note on the harness's premise: both opus re-runs reached a losing 'ending' (the Void after a fourth death), and the newcomer was unsure whether unhooding was an ending. No run reached a winning end, which Q4 does not yet allow.

- explorer-1 (partly): A Zork-style final test set by a silver-haired old man. A cliff with an unseen watcher, a hooded swordsman, a freezing lake, a grue passage, a rusted door. The mercy hint was noticed only after killing the figure.

- parser-breaker-1 (partly): A Zork-tradition cavern after a dream-test: a sword in a rock, a land of shadows, a hooded figure, an underground sea, and a death handled by an old man in a cell.
- prose-reader-1 (partly): A returning adventurer's final test at an endless stair, with a sword in a rock, a sunlit cliff oasis and an unseen figure toying with you from above.

### P1 · design · high · 3/8

Q3, which this round built, was barely played. Only explorer-1-opus got into the museum. No run went down into the Royal Puzzle, and no run pushed the gold machine. The other two runs that reached the Great Door came before the earthquake, found the iron door rusted shut and turned back for good. In 776 explorer-1-opus found the Jewel Room door locked and the south stairs ending blindly, and nothing pointed back to pushing the machine into the Jewel Room in the present. That covers all 36 puzzle squares, cp_out, the five rooms of the years between, old_jewel_room and the water slide; reach.handlers.never went from 17 to 59.

**Recommendation:** Give Q3's two gates a voice, in the way round 5 did for the repellent. Before the quake, the Great Door's rust, examined, should say the rock beside it is cracked and strained, so a visitor knows to come back. In 776, the locked stone door, or the guards' 'locked on their way out' line, should say the Jewel Room can only be reached from inside it. The present's gold machine, examined or pushed, should say it moves, for example scuffs on the floor or that it rolls a little at a touch. The hole at the puzzle entrance could say what lies below it (a ladder, a way out somewhere) so that 'can't climb back' is not the only thing it says.

### P3 · design · medium · 2/8

The Lake Shore's warning reads as a list of two things. It names 'your light first of all and your sword with it', so players left the light and the sword and went in with everything else. Explorer-1-opus lost the waybread (to mush) and the staff; goal-seeker-1-opus lost the staff and the vial. Both reports name this as the frustration.

**Recommendation:** Make the shore say everything: 'whatever you carry and cannot afford to lose to the bottom (everything in your hands, and your light first of all)'. Or name the waybread and the staff when they are carried. Leave the 'What you wear, it leaves you' clause as it is; that part works.

Evidence: explorer-1-opus turn 73 ("The waybread goes to mush in the water, and is gone."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 57 ("The shock of entering the frigid water has made you drop all your possessions into the lake!")

### P4 · design · medium · 1/8

The shore's told line 'a lit thing goes under here and comes up a dead one' teaches the wrong rule. Explorer-1-opus turned the lamp off to keep it and lost it anyway, and its report calls the warning misleading. The plunge line that followed has the switched-off lamp give 'a brief glow' as it goes under.

**Recommendation:** Say that any lamp dies in the water, lit or not ('a lamp goes under here, lit or dark, and comes up a dead one'). Keep the 'shore is where it should be left' clause.

Evidence: explorer-1-opus turn 207 ("a lit thing goes under here and comes up a dead one"); explorer-1-opus turn 208 ("The lamp is now off."); explorer-1-opus turn 210 ("Your lamp goes under with the rest. There is a hiss, a brief glow in the water below you, and then not.")

### P5 · world-bug · low · 1/8

Evidence: explorer-1-opus turn 210 ("There is a hiss, a brief glow in the water below you, and then not."); explorer-1-opus turn 217 ("it will not light again."); explorer-1-opus turn 218 ("Your lamp goes under with the rest. There is a hiss, a brief glow in the water below you, and then not.")

### P8 · design · medium · 2/8
