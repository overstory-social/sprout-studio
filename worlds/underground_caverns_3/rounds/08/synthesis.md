# Synthesis

**Legibility:** partly. The two opus runs, the only ones that went deep and died more than once, read the world almost exactly as intent.md has it. Both saw a final test set by an old man who is the Dungeon Master, won by gathering his things through patience and decency rather than force, with the visitor's own face under the hood, set in a ruined empire. Neither reached the end. explorer-1-opus died a fourth time in the mirror box with 7 of 7. goal-seeker-1-opus stopped on its last life after three deaths at the Guardians. The sonnet runs that got past Q1 (explorer-1, goal-seeker-1) saw the set pieces and the old man as the goal, but not that the hood, cloak, amulet, staff, book, ring and key are what he asks for: explorer-1 wished to 'learn what the ring and amulet are for'. The four short runs (casual, newcomer, parser-breaker, prose-reader) caught the dream frame and, where they fought, the doppelganger, but nothing of the arc. The melancholy came through in the deeper runs ('a sad tour of a collapsed empire'). The seven things become legible only after the cell vision.

- casual-1 (partly): A cave exploration game: seek the old man from the dream; wandering scenic tunnels, a cliff and a shore with no goal.
- newcomer-1 (partly): A last test before meeting a silver-haired old man; a sword in a rock and a con man on a cliff who tricks you out of a chest and gives you a staff.
- parser-breaker-1 (partly): A cave-adventure sequel: tested in a shadowy land, told to seek the old man when worthy; you fight a hooded double of yourself.
- prose-reader-1 (partly): The returning hero at an endless stair, told in a dream to seek a silver-haired man for a final test.
- explorer-1 (partly): A seeker on an old man's final test: a thief on a cliff, a duel with a shadow self, a lake that takes what you carry, a time machine, ending in a sliding-wall puzzle.
- goal-seeker-1 (partly): The aim is to reach the dream's old man past tests that each want a particular object or trick.
- explorer-1-opus (yes): An old adventurer's last test set by the Dungeon Master: collect what he carries by being good rather than forceful (let the thief rob you, feed the old man, spare the figure with your own face, hide a ring in history), on a sad tour of a collapsed empire.
- goal-seeker-1-opus (yes): An old man tests your worth and revives you after each death; you collect his staff, amulet, ring, book, hood, cloak and key through decency and patience, then face hard mechanical puzzles: the mirror box past the Guardians and the time machine.

## Points

### P1 · design · high · 2/8

No run reached the Treasury. Both opus re-runs got much further than the sonnet runs on their seeds: explorer-1-opus reached 7 of 7 and Q4, and goal-seeker-1-opus reached Q4's hallway. What stopped both was the mirror box. Each run did the same thing independently: raised the short pole, turned the box to north with the red panel, lowered the pole, then pushed the pine panel open and slid the box with the mahogany. The second slide, into the Guardians' stretch, killed them because the pine door was open. Nothing told either player that an open pine door can be seen from the hallway. The open state is one line in LOOK ('The pine panel has been opened outward.'), and the pine panel examined says only that the hallway shows through it. goal-seeker-1-opus then tried 'east' to go out through the pine panel and got 'You would hit one of the panels.', because with the arrow at north the pine panel faces south. 'Left' and 'right' in the box's description do not map to a compass direction a player can use. The reach metric agrees: front_door, behind_door, all four corridors, the parapet, the three cells, nirvana, mrg and mrd were never reached.

**Recommendation:** Keep the death and its odds, since they are the original's, but make the open door readable before it kills. While the box stands along the channel, the pine panel examined, and the line that opens it, should say that a door the hallway shows through is also a door the hallway sees through. The first slide made with a door open should mention the draught or the creak of the open pine on its hinges. The pine panel's description should also name the compass side it opens toward, read from :mdir, the same way the arrow is named.

Evidence: explorer-1-opus turn 403 ("lift short pole"); explorer-1-opus turn 407 ("push pine wall -> The pine wall swings open."); explorer-1-opus turn 410 ("Suddenly, two identical stone bludgeons come through the roof and hammer the contents of the box to pulp. That includes you."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 94 ("push pine panel -> The pine wall swings open."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 96 ("east -> You would hit one of the panels."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 98 ("Suddenly, two identical stone bludgeons come through the roof...")

### P2 · design · high · 1/8

A death inside the box leaves it parked in the Guardians' stretch, and after that the run is a trap that nothing explains. In goal-seeker-1-opus, after the turn-98 death the stretch south of the statues read 'divided by a wooden wall into small hallways to the northeast and northwest'. Walking northeast (turn 144) and northwest (turn 167) were instant deaths by the Guardians: the second and third deaths, and the third left one life. From outside the box, 'enter box', 'push panel' and 'open wooden wall' found no way in. The potion route, which the parked long-ways box actually opens, was there all along: the player had picked up the vial at turn 148 and examined it beside the statues at 143. Nothing pointed to it. The player stopped on the last life at turn 218.

**Recommendation:** This world has no restore, so decide whether a death in the box sends it back to where it starts, as a death sends the key home (log it in friction.md if so). If the box stays, the stretch south of the statues should say the small hallways run on between the Guardians' pedestals, and the wooden wall seen from outside should say the box is entered by its mirror and from nowhere else.

Evidence: goal-seeker-1-opus turn 141 ("The north side of the room is divided by a wooden wall into small hallways to the northeast and northwest."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 144 ("The Guardians awake, and in perfect unison, pulverize you with their bludgeons."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 163 ("enter box -> You hit your head against the wooden wall as you attempt this feat."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 166 ("open wooden wall -> You can't open the wooden wall."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 167 ("Three times now I have sent you back...")

### P3 · design · medium · 3/8

The potion's use is opaque, and players spend it or guess at it in the wrong places. goal-seeker-1 poured it on the cracked rock and threw the vial at it. explorer-1 tried 'put western wall in vial' and then poured the liquid on a puzzle wall. goal-seeker-1-opus examined the vial in front of the Guardians and walked into the narrow room unprotected the next turn. Every run that got the vial named it a puzzle without a lock.

**Recommendation:** Keep the original's riddle, but give the Guardians the other half of it. The statues examined, or the stretch south of them, should say they strike at what they see, in Zork's voice, so that a heavy, invisible, sweet-smelling liquid has something to connect to.

Evidence: goal-seeker-1 turn 46 ("pour vial on rock"); goal-seeker-1 turn 55 ("throw vial at rock"); explorer-1 turn 222 ("pour liquid on western wall -> It spills onto the western wall and vanishes."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 143 ("examine vial -> It is a small, transparent vial which looks empty but is strangely heavy."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 144

### P4 · world-bug · medium · 1/8

'drop all' reaches into an open vial and spills the potion. This happened at the Lake Shore, where the world's own text tells the visitor to leave everything in their hands on the shore. The player lost an endgame item to a bulk command the world had just recommended.

**Recommendation:** Make ALL for drop and put mean what is held directly, as Zork's ALL does, so that it never reaches the contents of a held container. A deliberate 'drop liquid' or 'pour vial' can still waste the potion.

Evidence: goal-seeker-1 turn 66 ("heavy but invisible liquid: Nothing seems to come out, although the sweet smell disappears from the vial..."); goal-seeker-1 turn 65 ("...the shore is where it should be left, with everything else in your hands.")

### P5 · world-bug · medium · 2/8

'pour <vial/liquid> on <scenery>' says the liquid 'spills onto X and vanishes', but the handler's :consume send reaches nothing (the engine warns), and the liquid stays in the vial. The prose and the state disagree: in goal-seeker-1 the inventory four turns after the pour still lists the liquid.

**Recommendation:** Have the pour act on the liquid itself, or on the vial's content, rather than sending to an item the target cannot reach. Then decide, as the intent's 'spilled, shaken out' suggests, that a pour wastes the potion, and say so in a line that matches the state.

Evidence: goal-seeker-1 turn 46 ("a send reached nothing: great_door.cracked_rock sent :consume to item, which reaches nothing in its range now"); goal-seeker-1 turn 50 ("The vial contains: A heavy but invisible liquid"); explorer-1 turn 222 ("a send reached nothing: cp_1.puzzle_west_wall sent :consume to item")

### P6 · world-bug · medium · 1/8

The world's new 'help' line no longer faults: round 7's fault is gone and faults went from 1 to 0. But the readings it offers include nonsense such as 'put eastern wall in vial', 'put walls in vial' and 'put lamp in vial', and the player acted on them. This breaks the brief's rule that a verb is offered only where something it applies to is in range.

**Recommendation:** Type put's item role by what can be carried (Portable) so scenery never fills it, or have help list only readings whose permit would allow. A short list of sensible readings is worth more than two dozen generated ones.

Evidence: explorer-1 turn 205 ("put eastern wall in vial, put western wall in vial, put northern wall in vial, put southern wall in vial, put walls in vial"); explorer-1 turn 219 ("put western wall in vial -> The vial isn't open."); explorer-1 turn 221 ("put western wall in vial -> You don't have that.")

### P7 · world-bug · medium · 1/8

The death line says what was carried lies 'somewhere along the way in, between the stair and the lake'. But the scatter code also sends things to the Damp Passage, the Dead End and the Tight Squeeze, which contradicts both that line and its own comment ('less the Tight Squeeze'). explorer-1-opus was wearing the ring at the third death, searched only the stair-to-lake rooms, and never found it again. The ring is one of the seven things.

**Recommendation:** Make the line and the list agree. Either name the passages east of the junction in the line ('along the ways in, from the stair to the lake and the passages east'), or drop the three rooms from the scatter, and fix the comment.

Evidence: explorer-1-opus turn 263 ("take ring -> You are already wearing it."); explorer-1-opus turn 323 ("...somewhere along the way in, between the stair and the lake, with the lamp alone brought back to your feet."); explorer-1-opus turn 326

### P8 · design · medium · 2/8

The Royal Puzzle is still where long runs stall. explorer-1 spent its last 53 turns there (from turn 182 to 235) and quit, saying the grid could not be mapped. explorer-1-opus believed it had jammed the grid, shouted, and took the new prayer exit at the cost of its third life. It did so while holding the book that pays the slot, which it had taken at turn 305. The new 'past saving' line never fired in that run, so either the grid was still solvable or the warning missed.

**Recommendation:** Replay explorer-1-opus's pushes from turn 284 to 322 and check whether the ladder was really past saving. If it was, the warning has a gap. If it was not, the shout's line should mention the slot and its fee before it mentions prayer, since a held book is a cheaper way out than a life.

Evidence: explorer-1 turn 182; explorer-1 turn 235; explorer-1-opus turn 305 ("take book"); explorer-1-opus turn 321 ("If you pray enough, it is said down here, your prayers may be answered."); explorer-1-opus turn 323 ("pray -> ...It costs what it costs.")

### P9 · design · low · 3/8

The early game gives a short session no hook toward the old man's things. casual quit at turn 10 and newcomer at turn 26 holding the staff, not knowing what it was for. The seven things become a goal only once the cell vision lists them, after a death. The staff's examine text says it looks like one 'seen before, in a dream', but the opening dream, which the brief fixes word for word, shows no staff.

**Recommendation:** Leave the dream as it is. Have the staff's own line, and the man's handing-over, point at the silver-haired old man the dream did show: the kind of staff such a man would lean on, worn to someone else's hand.

Evidence: casual-1 turn 10 ("I kept moving through rooms with no sense of purpose"); newcomer-1 turn 26 ("I wished I knew what the staff was for."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 25 ("...the look of one you have seen before, in a dream.")

### P10 · world-bug · low · 1/8

At the Cliff, with the watcher among the trees, 'hello, who is there?' drew Zork's random yuk 'Goodbye.' for the hello, then the who-is-there answer. The new watcher answers talk, examine and listen, but not hello, so the player read the yuk as a reply from the thing in the trees.

**Recommendation:** While the man is unmet, have hello at the Cliff reach the watcher with its slip back among the trunks, and keep the yuks for places where nobody could be listening.

Evidence: newcomer-1 turn 9 ("Goodbye. ... Nobody answers, which, down here, is the answer you were hoping for.")

### P11 · design · low · 3/8

Most of the round's refusals (refused rate 0 to 0.01) are 'wear X' answered 'You are already wearing it.' 'take all' does say 'You are now wearing the hood', but the amulet's take line ('It is a simple golden amulet!') never says it is put on, so players wear it again.

**Recommendation:** Have the amulet's take line end with it going round your neck, as the hood's and cloak's lines in take all do.

Evidence: explorer-1 turn 125 ("wear amulet -> You are already wearing it."); explorer-1-opus turn 387 ("wear hood -> You are already wearing it."); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 111 ("wear amulet -> You are already wearing it.")

### P12 · design · low · 4/5

Round 7's ledge and cliff changes work. Four of the five runs that tied the chest waited out the nine beats and were hauled up, and three quoted the beats or the watcher among their best lines. goal-seeker-1 scrambled down after five waits and lost the man, which is the told trap. The duel's turn to the hood also landed in every run that fought: parser-breaker, explorer-1 and explorer-1-opus.

**Recommendation:** Keep as it is.

Evidence: newcomer-1 turn 21 ("the sounds from above changed each time"); goal-seeker-1-opus turn 24 ("climb rope -> The man starts to heave on the rope"); goal-seeker-1 turn 26 ("scramble down"); parser-breaker-1 turn 14 ("recoil in horror at the sight of your own face")
