# underground_caverns_2: intent

_The author's. Sealed from playtesters; never quoted in the world._

## What this is

Zork II: The Wizard of Frobozz, ported as faithfully as Sprout allows,
a quarter at a time, and whole since round 7. The four quarters: the first
the brief names (the way in from the barrow to the garden, the Carousel
Room and Room 8, the Riddle Room and the Pearl Room, the well and the Tea
Room, the Posts Room and the Pool Room, and the robot's rooms), the
second (the ravine's ledge and the Tiny and Dreary Rooms, the dragon's
country from the Dragon Room to the Ice Room, and the Bank of Zork), the
third (the Lava Room and the Lava Tube, the Volcano View, the Cobwebby
Corridor, the Volcano Bottom and the volcano's air at four heights, the
Narrow Ledge and the Library, the Wide Ledge and the Dusty Room, the
Menhir Room and the Kennel, the Stairway and the nine Oddly-angled
Rooms), and the fourth, built in round 7 as the brief's schedule names it
and as round 5 built the third (the Guarded Room and its lizard, the
Wizard's Workshop, Workroom, Trophy Room and Quarters, the Pentagram Room
and the demon, the Aquarium Room and the Murky Room, the four Rooms of
Mist, the Cerberus Room, the Crypt Anteroom, the Crypt and the Landing).
Every room's name and description is the original's
from `2dungeon.zil` and `2actions.zil`, with a little more said where the
brief asks for it, in the same voice. The narrator is Zork's: "Taken.",
"Dropped.", the YUKS, the hollow laugh from the stone door.

Nothing carries over from Zork I. The lamp is "strangely familiar" and
that is all. The machinery (the adventurer, the verbs, darkness and the
grue, rooms that remember a visit) is adapted from the caverns world and
shares nothing by import.

The world is half a game and half an instrument: `friction.md` is where
every gap between Zork II and Sprout is written down, and that log is as
much the product as the rooms are.

## Where the quarter's boundary bends

- **The Wizard of Frobozz is in from round 1.** The brief allows him as
  late as round 3; he comes in now because the first quarter can carry
  him, and because a wandering spellcaster is the first of the showcase
  goals. He walks the whole built map on a clock of his own, every two
  minutes (four moves at the host's half minute a move) asking where the
  visitors are and rolling, one time in ten, to appear, as `I-WIZARD`
  rolls; all twelve spells are cast, and since round 3 Fierce has the
  dragon to bite on and Fireproof its fire. What he steals (Filch) goes to
  the trophy cabinet in his Trophy Room, which since round 7 is a room a
  visitor can stand in, and the cabinet a thing the demon can smash or the
  wand's own Filch can empty onto the floor. After round
  2.5, by the director's steering over my fidelity objections: his first
  call is certain, the first time his clock finds a visitor out of the
  barrow (the Narrow Tunnel or the bridge, in play), and he casts one of
  Feeble, Fumble and Fantasize, so the walk in has something in it
  besides walking; and in a visitor's first thirty moves he never casts
  Fence or Freeze, and any spell lasts the shortest three minutes. After
  round 3 the sword glows for him only when he lingers, as I-SWORD, run at
  the end of a move, would only ever have found him: a call that begins
  and ends in one move never reaches the sword, and the "very brightly /
  no longer glowing" flicker of one beat is gone (round 3's P9). After
  round 4 his first call carries the hook (P8): a line in the narrator's
  voice, not his, that reads off him what he is afraid of, that the
  treasures are south of the ravine and not yet found; and the first spell
  is drawn evenly among Feeble, Fumble and Fantasize. After round 5 (P5)
  the hook names the pearls, nearest, behind a stone door that asks a
  question, and "a room that will not keep still" between them and the
  rest, since three short runs quit at the Carousel having seen where but
  not what.
- **The unicorn is in**, since it is the garden's own and the sword glows
  for it; the gold key round its neck stays round its neck until the
  princess asks, which since round 3 she can. After round 1, when three
  runs hunted the key as the quarter's goal, the unicorn's and the key's
  close-ups say the key is being kept for somebody in particular, who is
  not you. After round 3 the north end keeps a stand-in for the unicorn
  while it is away, unseen while it is home, so "take key" the moment it
  has bounded off is answered in voice and not by the parser (P6). After
  round 4 (P5), when the explorer spent forty-five moves on it, the
  close-ups say the somebody is a lady and that she is not in this
  garden, and nothing done here will get the key off; "untie ribbon",
  "cut ribbon" and "take ribbon" are answered in that voice and without
  the melt. And the unicorn is gone for good once the princess has ridden
  it off, key given or not, as Zork's REMOVE takes it (P1): the stand-ins
  say so, and say whether the key went with her. The garden's clock holds
  the unicorn put for two moves after it comes or goes, since the roll
  alternated every other move (friction 43), and says "bounds lightly
  away" only where it can be seen to (P3). After round 5 (P8) the clock
  also holds it for the move a visitor arrives at the north end, so a
  unicorn the room lists is not announced arriving in the same breath.
- **The Deep Ford's ledge** was the edge of the first quarter; it leads up
  now, to the Ledge in Ravine.
- **The Rooms of Mist are built** (round 7): a death scatters what was
  carried as `RANDOMIZE-OBJECTS` scatters it (the lamp to the barrow, the
  gold key, the candy and the spheres to the Carousel Room, the Bank's
  money where it fell if it fell in the Bank, treasures to the Wizard's
  cabinet, the rest to the gazebo), and the dead walk west through red,
  blue and white mist, each a crystal sphere seen from inside with a
  vision of where the sphere's partner lies, to the black room, where the
  face sends them back to the barrow twice and keeps them the third time;
  and, with the demon loose in the Pentagram Room, keeps them at once, as
  DEAD-PALANTIR does. Before round 7 the face's words were said at the
  moment of death and the barrow came next.
- **The princess can be followed** (P10): every room on her way home
  keeps a stand-in, unseen while she is in it, that says which way she
  went from here, that she has not come this way yet, or how she left for
  good; and her "eyes fix on" line names the way out of the room she is
  spoken to in.
- **The Marble Hall's secret door** opens when the princess presses the
  loose marble on her way home, and stays open: east of the Marble Hall
  and west of the Path Near Stream lead through it from then on.
- **The Cobwebby Corridor is the third quarter's, not the fourth's.** The
  brief lists it among the Wizard's rooms; it is a corridor between two
  third-quarter places, the Carousel's southwest passage and the Lava
  Tube's crack, and it holds the fuse, without which the safe chain (the
  brick from the Marble Hall, the hole chipped in the safe, a match from
  the gazebo) would be split across the boundary. So it is built, with
  its southwest and down turning the visitor back at a fall of rock in
  Zork's voice; the Guarded Room beyond it is round 7's. With it and the
  Menhir Room built, every one of the Carousel's eight passages leads
  somewhere, and the spinning room's draw is Zork's seven.
- **The Kennel opens on the wand's Float** (round 7): the menhir, pointed
  at with the wand and told "float", rises for ten to twenty moves and
  the passage beneath it beckons; when the word runs out it sinks, and
  whoever is in the Kennel then is in the Kennel for good, unless the
  demon has moved it (his "move the menhir" lays it down beside the
  opening, "take the menhir" takes it away, "give me the menhir" drops it
  at his master's feet in the Pentagram Room). Until the wand is had,
  "southwest" from the Menhir Room is "You are trying to walk through an
  enormous rock.", as Zork's is.
- **The Oddly-angled Room at the foot of the stair has ten ways in
  Zork and eight here** (friction 58, 69): a place may have eight ways
  out, and its up to the Stairway and, since round 7, its down to the
  Cerberus Room must be two of them, so its southwest, which when the
  bases are run leads to a room with nothing in it but a window, and its
  west, which then leads to a corner the next corner also leads to, are
  left out. Up and down typed in the other eight rooms are "You can't go
  that way." rather than Zork's draw. After round 6 (P3, P4): the room at
  the foot of the stair keeps its stairway in its BRIEF entry, since
  named alone it looked like the other eight and players typed "up" in
  all of them; and the no-way draw, which walks the visitor a room along
  and back (friction 65) and so ends in a room heading, says that the
  passage let them out where they started.
- **Every way out leads somewhere** since round 7: the Cobwebby
  Corridor's southwest and down to the Guarded Room, and the secret
  passage down from the foot of the stair to the Cerberus Room, which
  were rockfalls in Zork's voice through round 6.
- **The gazebo's table carries what Zork's does**: the matchbook and the
  letter opener came in with round 3, since the opener is the Tiny Room's
  key to the key, and the matchbook is a light of six matches, two moves
  each, that the fuse and the balloon's fire want now.
- **The bucket can be waited for.** After round 2, when both sonnet runs
  poured the water in from outside and never saw the bucket again
  (its hundred moves are a hundred commands here, friction 37), "wait
  for the bucket" at the bottom of the well passes the hundred at a
  stroke, counts them on the score line, and brings it down empty. The
  loss is still Zork's loss; only the waiting is shorter. After round
  2.5, with the language's `pass`: plain "wait" is Zork's three moves,
  ninety seconds of the clock, so the Wizard comes to whoever waits; the
  bucket's hundred is a count of commands, three to a wait, and thirty-four
  waits bring it down; and the bucket poured into from outside strains at
  its rope for one move before it goes, and says so, so that whoever
  poured has one move to climb in. Its name stays behind at the end of the
  well it has left, so "enter bucket", "examine bucket" and "wait for
  bucket" are answered in voice while it is away. After round 3, when two
  long runs sat under the well reading "you could wait for the bucket" as
  description, the bottom's `waited` line and the absent bucket's close-up
  carry the command in Zork's parenthetical form, "(WAIT FOR THE BUCKET
  passes the whole of it at once.)", as the one-time BRIEF note already
  did (P1).
- **The carousel tells, a little.** Its draw is Zork's, but after round
  2 "examine passages" says the southeast one climbs toward a draught
  that smells of brick, and after the room has thrown a visitor out
  twice (three times before round 3, P13) its description says so too,
  and names the northwest passage's mist and the southwest one's cobwebs
  as well. After round 5 (P5) the first throw is followed, once, by a
  line in the narrator's voice that the floor has been turning under you
  and that a thing that is turned can be stopped by whoever finds the
  works. West while it spins is always a throw, as Zork's is; the two
  west exits are one now (friction 58), and a visitor who lands in Room 8
  with the room still spinning is thrown from there. After round 6 (P2,
  and the director's steering that a casual visitor should have something
  to want early), the room's own description, until it has thrown a
  visitor twice, says in the narrator's voice that the floor is turning,
  that every passage leads somewhere and the way to find out where is to
  walk into one, and that the one that climbs has a draught of old brick
  down it and, once, the grinding of a very heavy stone door: the door
  the hook promised. The first-throw line now says the throw was the
  floor's doing, rather than announcing the floor. Three rounds running,
  short runs quit on the move they entered it.
- **The cakes keep in the bucket** as they do at the Top of Well, since
  Zork's vehicle is in the room it stands in (round 3's P11), and the
  crumble line says why a cake crumbled. After round 5 (P6) the Posts
  Room says that whatever was left on the roof up there is out of reach,
  and the blue cake's close-up says it is small enough to pocket, which,
  for a cake laid beside one marked Eat Me, may be the point. After round
  6 (P5), when both runs that reached the table were stranded and both
  drowned, the tells come before the cost: the Eat-Me cake's close-up
  says the cake is half of the trick and the other half is on the same
  table, the table's says a guest who means to leave it their own size
  takes more than one cake, and the pool's close-up and "look under" say
  it is deeper than a swimmer and that whoever goes in does not come out.
  The drowning itself is Zork's, one command, as before.
- **The robot reaching for the sphere is Zork's** (round 5's P3): both
  opus runs sent the robot for the sphere to spare themselves the cage,
  and SPHERE-FCN has an answer for that, which is that the cage falls on
  the robot, the sphere is under it for good, and the visitor dies of
  grief or, Zork being Zork, of JIGS-UP. Ported as written: the closet
  says what lies under the cage, the robot's stand-ins say he has
  stopped humming, and the first quarter is then short a sphere.

## The arc a visitor walks

1. Inside the Barrow: the lamp and the sword on the ground, the door
   shut behind them. South, the Narrow Tunnel and the ravine, the Foot
   Bridge, the Great Cavern lit by moss, the Shallow Ford (water here, for
   the teapot), and the Dark Tunnel, the first dark room: the lamp must be
   on.
2. The garden: its north end with the gazebo (newspaper, place mat, china
   teapot, letter opener and matchbook on the tea table), the unicorn that
   comes and goes with a tiny gold key on a ribbon that cannot be had yet,
   the Formal Garden, the Topiary whose animals move when nobody is
   looking and, given long enough, attack. West of the Topiary, through
   the rose arbor, the Carousel Room. The newspaper, since round 3, has a
   second item: the Wizard, at large south of the ravine with the Empire's
   loose property, so that a short visit has something to want (P8).
3. The Carousel Room spins: every way out of it is a random one four
   times in five, with "You're not sure which direction is which." Its
   eight passages lead to the Marble Hall, the Path Near Stream, the
   Topiary, the Riddle Room, the Menhir Room, the Cobwebby Corridor, Room
   8 (only once it stops) and the Cool Room; the draw is Zork's seven.
4. The Riddle Room: "What is tall as a house, round as a cup, and all the
   king's horses can't draw it up?" Say "well" and the stone door opens
   (5 points). The Pearl Room beyond holds the pearl necklace (15).
5. The Circular Room, the bottom of the well, with the wooden bucket.
   Fresh water (the teapot filled at a ford) poured into the bucket makes
   it rise; in it, the visitor rises to the Top of Well (10 points).
   Salt water from the Pool Room does nothing. The water evaporates in a
   hundred moves and the bucket goes down again; taking the water out
   sends it down at once. Make a wish at the bottom with the priceless
   zorkmid on the floor and a whispering voice says what makes the bucket
   go, and the coin is gone.
6. The Tea Room: the oblong table with four cakes. The "Eat Me" cake
   makes the room huge (the visitor small): the Posts Room, where the
   table is four wooden posts and a roof and everything on it is too big
   to take, and east the Pool Room with the flask, the leak and the pool
   of tears. The red cake thrown in the pool evaporates it and shows the
   candy (15). The blue cake eaten in the Posts Room brings the visitor
   back to the Tea Room, everything restored; eaten anywhere else it
   crushes them. The orange cake explodes. The red one eaten dehydrates
   them. Read through the flask, the cakes say which is which. Cakes
   carried out of the wonderland crumble to dust.
7. The Low Room (northwest of the Tea Room), where the robot stands with
   its green paper; the Machine Room east of it, with three buttons a
   person is fried by and a robot is not; the Dingy Closet south, with
   the red crystal sphere (20). Reach for the sphere and a steel cage
   falls and gas comes in; the robot, if it is there, lifts the cage when
   told. Send the robot for the sphere instead and the cage has him. The
   triangular button stops the carousel (and brings the dented steel box
   with the Stradivarius (20) up through its floor); the square button
   speeds it, which makes the Low Room electric; the round button slows
   it again. With the carousel stopped, the Low Room is where a visitor
   cannot get their bearings, and Room 8 is where the grue repellent is.
   The robot says what it holds, in the room's list and when asked, and
   hands it over when asked for it (round 5's P3, P4).
8. The ravine (round 3): up from the Deep Ford, the Ledge in Ravine and
   the End of Ledge, where the air is warm and smells of smoke; up a
   precarious climb from the ledge, the Tiny Room, with the oak door, its
   barred window, a keyhole under a lid, and the Dreary Room beyond it lit
   red through a crack, where the blue crystal sphere (20) sits on a dusty
   table. The rusty key is in the far keyhole: open the lid, slide the
   place mat under the door, push the letter opener into the keyhole, and
   the key falls onto the mat; take the mat and the key falls to the
   floor; unlock the door and go through. Without the mat the key is lost.
9. The dragon's country: north from the End of Ledge, the Dragon Room,
   where the huge red dragon lies across the lair's mouth, watching.
   Examine it, greet it, strike it: it grows angry and follows, out of
   mingled curiosity and anger, as far as the Stone Bridge and the Cool
   Room, where its anger cools two a move; past seven it incinerates
   whoever provoked it. Since round 4 (P6) a blow's reply is graded rather
   than drawn (a shrug, a stare, and "You had better be very careful now"
   for the blow before the last), and when its interest runs out behind a
   visitor who has walked on, they hear it turn back. Led west into the
   Ice Room, it meets its own reflection in the glacier, breathes fire,
   and drowns in the melt (5 points); the dead dragon lies at the Deep
   Ford, the Ice Room's west passage opens on the Lava Room, and the lair
   is open: the rotten chest with the golden statuette (20), whose hinges
   give on the second try as the first try's line promises (P2), and the
   princess in a trance.
10. The princess: woken (examined, spoken to, or startled by the chest's
   hinges), she walks home a step a move, glancing back: the Dragon Room,
   the ledge, down to the Deep Ford, the Marble Hall, where she presses a
   loose piece of marble and the wall slides away, the Path Near Stream,
   the garden, the gazebo. Six moves after she is home, if the visitor is
   in the gazebo or at the garden's north end, the unicorn comes to her,
   she ties her hair with the ribbon, and gives the visitor the gold key
   (15) and a rose, and rides off; if nobody is there she leaves the rose
   in the gazebo and the key is gone for good, which is Zork's.
11. The Bank of Zork: west of the Dragon Room through the Fresco Room.
   The entrance, two tellers' rooms, two viewing rooms, the Safety
   Depository whose north wall is a curtain of light, and the Chairman's
   Office with his portrait (20). The curtain opens on a room chosen by
   the way the Depository was entered: from the West Teller's room, the
   East Viewing Room; from the East Teller's, the West; from the Office,
   the Small Room, which has no exits; and from the Small Room's south
   wall back into the Depository and through the curtain again, the
   Vault, with the bills (25). For twelve moves after a pass one wall of
   the room beyond leads back, and since round 5 (P2) the room says which
   wall shimmers while it does, a bare "walk through wall" takes it, and
   "Curtain Door Closed" says the way back has gone dark; then in the
   Small Room the Gnome of Zurich comes three moves later with a deposit
   box, takes a treasure for the way out, handed to him or put in his box
   (P1), and goes after twelve. The Depository's doors sound an alarm
   against the bills and the portrait; the curtain does not: carry them
   through it into a viewing room and walk out south. Since round 4 the
   alarm's line says it is the doorway that knows the Bank's property, and
   that the curtain was not built by the same people (P7).
12. The volcano (round 5): up the lava tube from the Ice Room to the
   Lava Tube, the Volcano View above it with the whole volcano in sight,
   and through its crack south the Cobwebby Corridor with the coil of
   black string, which also leads northeast to the Carousel. West from the
   Ice Room, once the glacier has gone, the Lava Room with the moby ruby
   (15) and south of it the Volcano Bottom, where the balloon stands: a
   wicker basket, a cloth bag, a metal receptacle shut in the middle, and
   a braided wire. Open the receptacle, put something burnable in it (the
   newspaper burns a hundred moves, the blue label that drops from the
   bag twenty, a book two hundred), burn it with a match, and the bag
   fills; every third move the balloon rises while the receptacle is open
   and the fire burning, and sinks while it is shut or the fire out.
   Beside the Narrow Ledge, "west" or "land" brings it down: the ledge
   has a hook to tie the wire to (three moves, or the balloon goes on
   without you), the priceless zorkmid (20) on its floor, and the Library
   south of it, where the purple book has a Flathead stamp (10) in it.
   Beside the Wide Ledge, the same: the hook, and south the Dusty Room
   with the rusty safe, an oblong hole chipped in its front, and inside
   it Lord Dimwit's crown (20) and a foreman's card. The brick from the
   Marble Hall goes in the hole, the string in the brick, a match to the
   string, and the blast is at the end of the next move: it opens the
   safe, and five moves later brings the room down, and eight after that
   the ledge, so the crown is taken quickly and the balloon untied and
   away before the ledge goes. Above the Wide Ledge the balloon floats out
   of the volcano for good, and whoever rides it dies in the Flathead
   Mountains; shut the receptacle to come down, and keep the fire lit
   until the bottom, since a balloon that lands without one is pieces.
   Left on a ledge by a balloon that floated off, the visitor is found
   ten moves later by the Volcano Gnome, who opens a door on the west end
   of the ledge for a treasure, crunches anything else, and leaves for
   good if given the brick.
13. The Menhir (round 5): south of the Carousel, the Menhir Room, a
   quarry with one great menhir carved with an F leaning across the way
   southwest to the Kennel and the collar (15, round 7's). South again
   the Stairway, and down it the Oddly-angled Rooms, nine of them, with a
   diamond-shaped window in each floor that glows by how far the runner
   has got. From the room at the foot of the stair any way is a draw: a
   third of the time no way, a sixth a throw into one of the rooms that
   are not corners, and half a throw into a corner, where the wooden club
   burned "Babe Flathead" lies the first time. From a corner, the bases
   are run as a runner runs them, southeast to first, northeast to
   second, northwest to third, southwest home, each the next corner round
   the diamond; a wrong way is a throw, and twenty wrong ways earn the
   Wizard's taunt. Home, a rusty squeal echoes (5 points), the floor at
   the foot of the stair swings down on a passage into unrelieved
   darkness, which ends in a fall of rock for now, and the rooms join as
   Zork joins them.
14. The Wizard's rooms (round 7): southwest from the Cobwebby Corridor,
   the Guarded Room, with a stained and battered door at its south end
   and a lizard's head imbedded in the door that snaps at a hand on the
   handle or a key in the lock; fed the candy, it sleeps, and the gold
   key the princess gave unlocks the door. South, the Wizard's Workshop
   (10 points); west of it the Workroom, where the workbench carries
   three stands, ruby, sapphire and diamond, and a sheet of notes with a
   drawing of three circles round a black fourth; south of the Workshop
   the Trophy Room, with the degree, the wands, the swords, the bottles,
   the owl, the warning label, and the ornate cabinet imbedded in the
   wall, under a fearful spell, holding whatever Filch and the dead gave
   up. West of the Workroom the Aquarium Room, where a baby sea serpent
   writhes in a huge tank with a clear crystal sphere in its sand: a
   weapon or anything heavy thrown or swung at the glass breaks it, the
   serpent dies on the floor, and the sphere (20) can be had; "break" the
   tank, as against "attack" or "throw", and the serpent takes you with
   it, as AQUARIUM-FCN has it. "In" is the Murky Room, and the end of
   whoever goes in. South of the Aquarium Room, the Wizard's Quarters, a
   different room every time it is looked at, never the same twice
   running. The red sphere on the ruby stand, the blue on the sapphire
   and the clear on the diamond: three puffs of smoke, and a black stand
   of obsidian with a black sphere (30) in it. South of the Workroom, the
   Pentagram Room: the black sphere put in the circle calls the demon,
   and the Wizard materializes at once (Zork gives him up to four moves),
   his clock stopped and his wand powerless. The demon's fee is ten
   treasures (2 points each), counting what the Wizard's cabinet holds,
   which is what the cabinet is for; paid, he does one thing: "demon,
   give me the wand" (the wand, 30, and the Wizard runs for good),
   "demon, kill the wizard" (the same, less politely), "demon, move the
   menhir", "demon, take the menhir", "demon, give me the menhir",
   "demon, kill cerberus" (too much for him), "demon, open the case"
   (smashed, and everything in it), "demon, give me X" for anything
   portable in the room, "demon, kill me". Before the fee, "My fee is
   not paid!"; the brick as a gift breaks his contract and he goes.
15. The wand in hand (round 7): pointed at a thing ("wave wand at X",
   "point wand at X", "touch X with wand"), it glows for two moves, and a
   word said over it ("float", "say float", "incant float", and the
   rest) runs for ten to twenty moves: Float lifts the menhir, or a
   portable thing out of reach, or the dog an inch; Filch brings a
   portable thing to hand, or empties the Wizard's cabinet onto the
   floor; Fry is the end of a portable thing; Fluoresce makes a thing
   glow; Fudge smells of chocolate; Fierce on the dog is the end of you;
   the other F-words have no obvious effect. Pointed again before the
   word has run out, a lecture, or one time in twenty the end. The white
   book in the Library says how, as it always did.
16. The crypt (round 7): the secret passage down from the foot of the
   stair, once the bases are run, to the Cerberus Room (10), where the
   three-headed dog guards the arch east; the collar from the Kennel put
   on it ("put collar on dog", "give collar to dog") makes it a dog, and
   taking the collar back is the end of you. East, the Crypt Anteroom
   (3), "Feel Free" over a marble door that squeaks; south, the Crypt
   (2), the twelve Flatheads' heads on poles, which punish a hand laid on
   them, and the carved crypt; with the lamp turned off, a faint outline
   on the south wall with an F in it, a secret door that opens
   noiselessly; south, the Landing, where the green lines read whoever
   stands there: with the wand, the end of Zork II and 400 of 400;
   without, the wardens, and the Rooms of Mist.
17. The Wizard, every two minutes, one time in ten: he appears, waves his
   wand, speaks a word beginning with "F", and vanishes cackling. Feeble,
   Fumble, Fear, Filch, Freeze, Fall, Ferment, Fierce, Float, Fireproof,
   Fence, Fantasize: each changes what the visitor can do for three to
   eighteen minutes (five to thirty-five moves), and ends with its own
   line. Fence puts a fence in the room that can be examined, pushed and
   walked into. A spell is timed by the clock rather than by moves, so
   that one which refuses every move still runs out; "wait" runs the
   clock ninety seconds. Fantasize, after round 2.5, is not Zork's
   one-in-five flicker on every description: each room rolls once when
   the spell is cast, one room in two gets one of the eight, and it stays
   listed with what is really there until the spell ends. Filch, since
   round 5 (P9), says how many things went, and the first thing that
   vanishes at a touch names the Wizard's word as the cause; since round
   6 (P6) it says they went where the Wizard keeps what he takes. Since
   round 6 (P15) Freeze and Float, the two that refuse every move, draw
   their length from the short half of the range (three to ten minutes),
   and Float's refusal points at "wait" as Freeze's does.

"Done" is Zork II done: the riddle answered, the well risen, the pearl,
the candy, the sphere and the violin taken, the robot told what to do and
doing it, the carousel stopped, the dragon drowned, the statuette, the
gold key, the portrait, the bills and the blue sphere taken, the ruby, the
zorkmid, the stamp and the crown taken, the bases run, the lizard fed and
the door unlocked, the Workshop entered, the aquarium broken and the clear
sphere taken, the three spheres made the black one, the demon called and
paid ten treasures and the wand had off the Wizard, the menhir floated
and the collar taken, the dog leashed, the anteroom and the crypt
entered, and the Landing reached with the wand in hand: 400 of 400, and
the rank of Master Adventurer without the "but somehow you don't feel
done" that 400 points short of the Landing earn. The ceiling line of
rounds 3 to 6 is gone with the ceiling.

## Secrets and interactions I hope players find

- **The carousel never sends you west** while it spins, so Room 8 and the
  repellent can only be found once the robot has pushed the triangular
  button. The dull thump heard in the distance is the steel box arriving.
- **The Low Room turns the tables**: once the carousel is stopped, the
  Low Room is the one that scrambles the compass, and with the square
  button pushed it kills. A player who pushes everything the robot can
  push learns the order matters.
- **The cage is a trap with a tell**: the robot, if it was in the closet,
  is caught in the cage too, bent double and humming, and the description
  says so; a player who reads it knows whom to ask. Since round 3 the cage
  has a close-up of its own (the bars nobody could lift, the gas coming
  in, the robot's absence remarked on), the gas thickens a line a move,
  and an order to the robot while it stands in another room is answered
  in voice by its stand-in, which says where it is (P2, P3). Since round
  5 the stand-in answers an order to go as well (P4), and the robot sent
  for the sphere instead of you is the robot's last errand. The stand-in
  answers a look with the same line since round 6 (P14), reworded to fit
  one ("not here, to be looked at or to hear you"), because its `any`
  refusal cannot let a look through (friction 67).
- **Dying costs more than ten points**: the lamp goes back to the barrow,
  treasures to the Wizard's stash (out of reach until his rooms are
  built), and everything else to the gazebo; the third death is the end.
  Since round 6 (P6) the demon's return says where it all went, and that
  what was worth anything is kept by the Wizard somewhere not yet seen;
  Filch's count says the same of what it took. A player who knows the
  treasures are kept has a reason to find his rooms in round 7.
- **The sword glows** blue near the unicorn, in its room as next door
  since round 4 (P13: five runs read the bright glow as the Wizard), and
  brightly when the Wizard stands in the room and stays; it is the only
  warning of either. (It never did glow for the Wizard before round 2.5:
  a visitor is sent no `:arrived`, friction 40; he tells the sword himself
  now, and only when he lingers.)
- **The cakes crumble** carried out of the wonderland, so the flask must
  be read in the Pool Room, not in the lamp-light of the Carousel.
- **The bucket keeps the water**: climb in before pouring, or watch it go
  up without you ("The bucket swiftly rises up, and is gone."), and wait
  a hundred moves for it to come back. The tells, added after round 1
  when nobody raised it: a tide-line inside the bucket that says it has
  held water lately; the rope, taut, going up into the dark, which `look
  up` and the rope's own description point at; and, once the bucket has
  gone up alone, a line in the room that whatever hauled it up will
  presumably let it down again. After round 2, when the tells were read
  and did not help: the bucket's close-up, seen from outside, says it
  goes up with what is in it and without what is not; "wait" at the
  bottom while it is up says the wait is possible and long; "wait for
  the bucket" does it; and the rope, climbed or pulled at either end,
  answers in voice. After round 2.5 the pour from outside is warned
  before it costs: the rope goes taut and the bucket lifts at one side
  and "has not gone yet", and a visitor who climbs in on that move rises
  with it. After round 3 the wait line names the command.
- **The topiary animals** move when the visitor has stood too long among
  them, and close in, and then attack. Leave when they move. Each of
  them (the dragon, the serpent, the dog, the human figures) has a
  close-up of its own since round 2, and each carries the tell; the
  unicorn among them is hedge, and says so.
- **The Fantasize spell's things** ("There is a basilisk here.") are
  every room's own, beside the grue: reaching for one is answered in the
  narrator's voice, so a player can tell an illusion from a parser
  failure (round 2's P5). Since round 2.5 each stays put for the length
  of the spell, listed on arrival and on "look", and a room's illusion
  answers to its words only while it is shown; the copy of ZORK I answers
  to "zork" since round 3 (P4); the yellow sphere beside the closet's real
  one is a coin-toss on "take sphere", which is the Wizard's joke and not
  mine. Since round 4 the refusal opens with the verb's own clause, an eye
  for looking and a blow for striking (P11), and the grue the spell shows
  is answered for by the room's grue, which says that a grue in plain
  sight is the one thing no grue has ever been (P12).
- **Brief mode is Zork's**: a room seen again is named and no more. The
  first time it happens, one parenthetical line says LOOK, VERBOSE and
  BRIEF, and is not said again.
- **"Examine room" is "look"** since round 6 (P11): every room holds a
  thing called "room" (and "chamber", "place", "surroundings") whose
  close-up is the room's own description, because the room's own name
  words are refused wherever anything in it answers better. The Pool
  Room's "hazy something" can be examined too, and is answered with the
  pool's close-up until the pool is gone.
- **The Wizard's spells interact**: Feeble makes the load fifty; Fumble
  drops things from a full hand; Float puts the floor out of reach and,
  at the Top of Well, ends in a long fall; Fear sends the visitor fleeing
  by a random way, which in the Carousel Room is random twice over, and
  from the dragon as from the Wizard; Fierce turns the visitor on the
  dragon, which is a way to anger it; Fireproof makes the dragon's fire
  wash over harmlessly, once, and the doubling-back dragon crushes a
  fireproof visitor in its jaws instead; Filch makes anything of value
  vanish as it is touched; Fantasize puts things in rooms that are not
  there, the volcano's air included.
- **"Robot, go east"** works, comma and all, and so does "tell the robot
  to push the triangular button"; the robot answers "Whirr, buzz, click!"
  and does it, or, now and then, "Buzz! Buzz! Buzz! My circuits are
  getting rusty. Try again."
- **Taking the sphere is a trap** only if the robot is not there to lift
  the cage; a player who sends the robot south first is safe, and one who
  does not has six moves of poison gas to think it over. A player who
  sends the robot for the sphere has read the wrong page of the manual.
- **Make a wish at the bottom of the well** with the priceless zorkmid
  lying there and a whispering voice tells you what makes the bucket go;
  unfortunately, wishing makes the coin go.
- **The dragon's anger is arithmetic**: examine it for one, greet it for
  two, strike or kick it or throw at it for four, give to it for one; it
  cools by two a move; past six it kills. So two blows and a walk lose it
  by the Cool Room, and the way to the glacier is to strike it again on
  the bridge and again in the Cool Room, so that it is still interested
  when it sees itself. Sneak past it to the lair and it doubles back.
  Since round 6 (P8) the "interest runs out" line is heard in the Ice
  Room as well as in the dragon's own country, which is where a visitor
  who kicked it twice and walked straight on is standing when it gives up.
- **The dragon takes gifts**: a treasure given to it is "excused for a
  moment" and goes into the chest in its lair, where it can be had back
  once the dragon is dead; anything else is refused.
- **The princess will not be touched**: strike her and the Wizard, of all
  people, unrolls himself out of nothing and says "Fry!".
- **The princess is a clock**: she walks a step a move, three moves in
  four, and six moves after she is home the unicorn comes. A visitor who
  dawdles finds a rose in the gazebo and no key, ever. Follow her, and
  wait in the Marble Hall for the wall to open if you are ahead.
- **The Bank's brochure tells the whole trick** if read slowly: the tellers
  "seem to walk through walls". The way out with the money is through the
  curtain into a viewing room, not through a door; and the curtain opens
  on a viewing room only when the Depository was entered from a teller's
  room, so the loot must be set down, the room left and re-entered, and
  the loot picked up again.
- **The Small Room is a trap with a price**: the way in is the Office,
  and the way out, once the curtain has closed, is the south wall while it
  still shimmers, or the gnome, who takes a treasure for it, by hand or
  into his box. Throw the loot through a wall of light and it lands in the
  Depository; through the curtain, and it lands in the room the curtain
  opens on, and the curtain closes behind it.
- **The key without the mat is lost**: the push sends it somewhere that
  is not a room, and the game says nothing, as Zork says nothing. The mat
  pulled from under the door, rather than taken, is drawn out and the key
  falls off it (round 4's P9). Since round 6 (P13) "unlock door" with the
  rusty key in hand and none named uses it, "(with the rusty iron key)",
  as Zork's parser would have implied it; the visitor's own part of
  `unlock` tells the door whether the key is held (friction 61 again).
- **The bucket's tide-line says what water it wants**: fresh, the kind the
  stream has at a ford and a teapot carries (round 4's P4).
- **The matchbook is a light**: six matches, two moves each, for a visitor
  who has lost the lamp to the Wizard; and the only flame in the domain,
  for the fuse and the balloon's fire. A match struck is good for the
  next command and no more.
- **Two keys answer to "key"**: with both in hand the parser draws, and
  tells you which it drew; say "iron key" or "gold key".
- **The receptacle is the balloon's whole control**: open, with a fire in
  it, the balloon rises; shut, it sinks, smoke leaking round the lid;
  the fire out, it sinks and does not survive the ground. Everything else
  typed in the basket is "You can't control the balloon this way." The
  blue label that drops from the bag says LAND, and LAND beside a ledge
  is the only landing there is; the viewing ledge is too thin.
- **The books are fuel**: the white one, which is the only one small
  enough for the receptacle, burns a hundred moves, and the Library is on
  the first ledge, so a visitor whose newspaper is nearly out can refuel
  there; the purple one, read, falls open on the stamp.
- **The brick has other properties than weight**: burn it in your hand
  and it says so, once. Light the fuse in it anywhere and the room it is
  in is wrecked in one move and comes down in five; whoever is in the
  room with it goes with it. In the hole in the safe it opens the safe,
  and the Dusty Room's card, read too late, says the room was built on
  weak rock. Since round 6 (P7) the blast heard from outside says the rock
  has not stopped moving and what is wanted from in there wants getting
  quickly, and "wait" while any of the volcano's countdowns runs says it
  is spending three moves. The window is still Zork's five, and a wait
  inside it still costs three of them; a visitor who waits, walks in and
  takes the crown dies with it, in Zork as here.
- **The Wide Ledge collapses** thirteen moves after the blast, and a
  balloon tied to it goes down with it, rider and all; untied, it is left
  "with no place to land", and floats off.
- **The Volcano Gnome is an alarm clock**: he comes ten moves after the
  balloon leaves you on a ledge, grows nervous when talked to, and is
  late for his appointment five moves after that. His fee is any
  treasure; his door leads down a chimney to the Volcano Bottom. Give
  him the brick and he is never seen again.
- **The demon's fee counts the cabinet** (round 7): every treasure the
  Wizard has filched or the dead gave up sits in his trophy cabinet, under
  a spell no hand gets through, and every one of them counts toward the
  ten the demon asks, as CASE-WORTH counts them. So a visitor who has lost
  treasures to him has not lost them to the fee, and once the wand is had,
  "wave wand at cabinet" and "filch" lays the lot at their feet. "Demon,
  open the case" smashes it and everything in it, which is Zork's joke.
- **The lizard eats what it is given**: the candy puts it to sleep; a
  sphere it cannot chew and spits out; the brick goes down and pops; the
  flask it will not have; anything else is crunched and gone.
- **The black sphere frightens the Wizard**: with it in hand his clock
  finds you and he leaves in unseemly haste, and each of the other
  spheres carried takes a fifth off his will to cast (I-WIZARD's
  CAST-PROB).
- **The menhir sinks**: Float runs ten to twenty moves, and a visitor in
  the Kennel when the word runs out is walled in, unless the demon has
  laid the menhir down for good. The demon's "give me the menhir" drops it
  at your feet in the Pentagram Room, which is funnier than useful.
- **The aquarium answers to how it is broken**: thrown at or attacked with
  a sword, the serpent dies short of your foot; "break the aquarium with
  the sword", and it rends you first. The flask thrown at the glass is
  poison gas for everybody.
- **The crypt's door shows in the dark**: the room is lit only by what a
  visitor brings, and the F on the south wall only when they bring
  nothing; "turn off lamp", and the door is there to be opened. The heads
  on the poles are not to be touched, and say so once.
- **The Landing reads the hands**: the wand in them is the way down;
  empty, the green lines are the end, and the Rooms of Mist again.
- **The bat says who to be**: "Babe Flathead", burned into a club lying
  by the first base, is the hint that the Oddly-angled Rooms are a
  diamond to be run, southeast and round. The windows glow brighter base
  by base, and go dark when the count is lost. After round 6 (P3), when
  no run in two rounds had examined the club, the name is on the club's
  room line, first description and plain, and the window's close-up says
  it glows by a count being kept, one step a grade, until what is counted
  comes round to where it began. The room lines keep Zork's own words for
  the grades. After round 7 (P1), when three runs could not tell a throw
  from a step: a corner has a square canvas bag nailed to its floor, a
  base, and its close-up says a runner touches the next one on round; the
  club's close-up says a bat is for running the bases with, first to home;
  the window's close-up names the grades as bases, a flicker for first and
  brightest for home; and the move itself says what it was, in the
  narrator's voice: a step from a corner the right way "runs straight, for
  once", a wrong one "turns more corners than it has any right to" and
  loses the count, and a draw into a corner from the room at the foot of
  the stair says the window has woken.
- **The whirring is the pointer** (round 7's P2): four short runs quoted
  the hook and turned east into the garden one move short of the
  Carousel, so the hook says the room that will not keep still is heard
  before it is seen, the Path Near Stream's and the Marble Hall's whirring
  lines say it is something very large that will not keep still, and the
  newspaper's Wizard item mentions the whirring and the room. A pointer,
  not a map.
- **The glacier says what it wants** (round 7's P5): burned, with a match
  or without, it says it would take a great deal more heat than that, the
  kind that comes out of something's mouth; the teapot poured over it
  freezes on the face of it and says water is not what it wants (the
  teapot pours now, over a thing and into the bucket, and the hand that
  holds it does the pouring, since it is the only thing that can see the
  water in it, friction 61 again); and the dragon's close-up says he has
  caught sight of himself in the brass of the lamp and does not care for
  the other dragon in it.
- **The princess, followed late** (round 7's P6): her "eyes fix on" line
  in the lair names the blackened doorway to the south; her turn and the
  dragon's are told the room the visitor ends the move in rather than the
  one the Carousel's exit put them in before the throw (friction 74); and
  once she has ridden off without giving the key, the north end and the
  gazebo say so in their own descriptions, a hoofprint and a hedge pushed
  through and nothing that says she waited.
- **The wall of light is a way** (round 7's P8): while a wall of a room
  beyond the curtain shimmers, its direction leads back to the Depository,
  and "curtain" and "light" name the shimmer, which answers "enter" as the
  wall does.
- **The balloon says where the top is** (round 7's P7): near the Wide
  Ledge the air says a balloon that rises from here rises out of the
  volcano for good, and the ledge is the last place to come down. The
  death itself, and what it costs, is Zork's.
- **The teller rooms name the way back** (round 7's P13), the diamond
  rooms' walls are the room's own wall's to describe (P10), the bucket
  answers "look in" as a container (P9), "pry" and "force" are "open"
  (P12), the floor is no longer "stone" so "stone door" does not find it
  (P12), "take all" from a hand too full for the engine's eight says so
  (P11, friction 73), and a visitor who dies in the basket is out of it
  when they wake (P4).
