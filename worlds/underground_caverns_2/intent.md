# underground_caverns_2: intent

_The author's. Sealed from playtesters; never quoted in the world._

## What this is

Zork II: The Wizard of Frobozz, ported as faithfully as Sprout allows,
a quarter at a time. After round 3 two quarters stand: the first the brief
names (the way in from the barrow to the garden, the Carousel Room and
Room 8, the Riddle Room and the Pearl Room, the well and the Tea Room, the
Posts Room and the Pool Room, and the robot's rooms) and the second (the
ravine's ledge and the Tiny and Dreary Rooms, the dragon's country from
the Dragon Room to the Ice Room, and the Bank of Zork). Every room's name
and description is the original's from `2dungeon.zil` and `2actions.zil`,
with a little more said where the brief asks for it, in the same voice.
The narrator is Zork's: "Taken.", "Dropped.", the YUKS, the hollow laugh
from the stone door.

Nothing carries over from Zork I. The lamp is "strangely familiar" and
that is all. The machinery (the adventurer, the verbs, darkness and the
grue, rooms that remember a visit) is adapted from the caverns world and
shares nothing by import.

The world is half a game and half an instrument: `friction.md` is where
every gap between Zork II and Sprout is written down, and that log is as
much the product as the rooms are.

## Where the quarter's boundary bends

- **The Wizard of Frobozz is in from round 1.** The brief allows him as
  late as round 3; he comes in now because the first quarter can carry
  him, and because a wandering spellcaster is the first of the showcase
  goals. He walks the whole built map on a clock of his own, every two
  minutes (four moves at the host's half minute a move) asking where the
  visitors are and rolling, one time in ten, to appear, as `I-WIZARD`
  rolls; all twelve spells are cast, and since round 3 Fierce has the
  dragon to bite on and Fireproof its fire. What he steals (Filch) goes to
  a stash that will become his Trophy Room's case in round 7. After round
  2.5, by the director's steering over my fidelity objections: his first
  call is certain, the first time his clock finds a visitor out of the
  barrow (the Narrow Tunnel or the bridge, in play), and he casts one of
  Feeble, Fumble and Fantasize, so the walk in has something in it
  besides walking; and in a visitor's first thirty moves he never casts
  Fence or Freeze, and any spell lasts the shortest three minutes. After
  round 3 the sword glows for him only when he lingers, as I-SWORD, run at
  the end of a move, would only ever have found him: a call that begins
  and ends in one move never reaches the sword, and the "very brightly /
  no longer glowing" flicker of one beat is gone (round 3's P9). After
  round 4 his first call carries the hook (P8): a line in the narrator's
  voice, not his, that reads off him what he is afraid of, that the
  treasures are south of the ravine and not yet found; and the first spell
  is drawn evenly among Feeble, Fumble and Fantasize.
- **The unicorn is in**, since it is the garden's own and the sword glows
  for it; the gold key round its neck stays round its neck until the
  princess asks, which since round 3 she can. After round 1, when three
  runs hunted the key as the quarter's goal, the unicorn's and the key's
  close-ups say the key is being kept for somebody in particular, who is
  not you. After round 3 the north end keeps a stand-in for the unicorn
  while it is away, unseen while it is home, so "take key" the moment it
  has bounded off is answered in voice and not by the parser (P6). After
  round 4 (P5), when the explorer spent forty-five moves on it, the
  close-ups say the somebody is a lady and that she is not in this
  garden, and nothing done here will get the key off; "untie ribbon",
  "cut ribbon" and "take ribbon" are answered in that voice and without
  the melt. And the unicorn is gone for good once the princess has ridden
  it off, key given or not, as Zork's REMOVE takes it (P1): the stand-ins
  say so, and say whether the key went with her. The garden's clock holds
  the unicorn put for two moves after it comes or goes, since the roll
  alternated every other move (friction 43), and says "bounds lightly
  away" only where it can be seen to (P3).
- **The Deep Ford's ledge** was the edge of the first quarter; it leads up
  now, to the Ledge in Ravine.
- **The Rooms of Mist are not built** (they are the end's, round 7), so a
  death is answered with the demon's own words from the fourth Room of
  Mist, said at once, and the visitor wakes in the barrow as Zork's
  `GOTO INSIDE-BARROW` leaves them, their things scattered as
  `RANDOMIZE-OBJECTS` scatters them: the lamp to the barrow, treasures to
  the Wizard's stash, the rest to the gazebo. A third death is the end.
- **The princess can be followed** (P10): every room on her way home
  keeps a stand-in, unseen while she is in it, that says which way she
  went from here, that she has not come this way yet, or how she left for
  good; and her "eyes fix on" line names the way out of the room she is
  spoken to in.
- **The Marble Hall's secret door** opens when the princess presses the
  loose marble on her way home, and stays open: east of the Marble Hall
  and west of the Path Near Stream lead through it from then on.
- **The ways out of the built quarters** (south and southwest of the
  Carousel Room; west and up from the Ice Room, where the Lava Room and
  the Lava Tube are the volcano's) are present and turn the visitor back
  in Zork's voice, each rockfall its own. The Carousel's northwest passage
  leads to the Cool Room now, and the spinning room's draw is among five.
- **The gazebo's table carries what Zork's does**: the matchbook and the
  letter opener came in with round 3, since the opener is the Tiny Room's
  key to the key, and the matchbook is a light of six matches, two moves
  each, that round 5's fuse will want.
- **The bucket can be waited for.** After round 2, when both sonnet runs
  poured the water in from outside and never saw the bucket again
  (its hundred moves are a hundred commands here, friction 37), "wait
  for the bucket" at the bottom of the well passes the hundred at a
  stroke, counts them on the score line, and brings it down empty. The
  loss is still Zork's loss; only the waiting is shorter. After round
  2.5, with the language's `pass`: plain "wait" is Zork's three moves,
  ninety seconds of the clock, so the Wizard comes to whoever waits; the
  bucket's hundred is a count of commands, three to a wait, and thirty-four
  waits bring it down; and the bucket poured into from outside strains at
  its rope for one move before it goes, and says so, so that whoever
  poured has one move to climb in. Its name stays behind at the end of the
  well it has left, so "enter bucket", "examine bucket" and "wait for
  bucket" are answered in voice while it is away. After round 3, when two
  long runs sat under the well reading "you could wait for the bucket" as
  description, the bottom's `waited` line and the absent bucket's close-up
  carry the command in Zork's parenthetical form, "(WAIT FOR THE BUCKET
  passes the whole of it at once.)", as the one-time BRIEF note already
  did (P1).
- **The carousel tells, a little.** Its draw is Zork's, but after round
  2 "examine passages" says the southeast one climbs toward a draught
  that smells of brick, and after the room has thrown a visitor out
  twice (three times before round 3, P13) its description says so too,
  and names the northwest passage's mist as well.
- **The cakes keep in the bucket** as they do at the Top of Well, since
  Zork's vehicle is in the room it stands in (round 3's P11), and the
  crumble line says why a cake crumbled.

## The arc a visitor walks

1. Inside the Barrow: the lamp and the sword on the ground, the door
   shut behind them. South, the Narrow Tunnel and the ravine, the Foot
   Bridge, the Great Cavern lit by moss, the Shallow Ford (water here, for
   the teapot), and the Dark Tunnel, the first dark room: the lamp must be
   on.
2. The garden: its north end with the gazebo (newspaper, place mat, china
   teapot, letter opener and matchbook on the tea table), the unicorn that
   comes and goes with a tiny gold key on a ribbon that cannot be had yet,
   the Formal Garden, the Topiary whose animals move when nobody is
   looking and, given long enough, attack. West of the Topiary, through
   the rose arbor, the Carousel Room. The newspaper, since round 3, has a
   second item: the Wizard, at large south of the ravine with the Empire's
   loose property, so that a short visit has something to want (P8).
3. The Carousel Room spins: every way out of it is a random one four
   times in five, with "You're not sure which direction is which." Its
   eight passages lead to the Marble Hall, the Path Near Stream, the
   Topiary, the Riddle Room, the Cool Room, Room 8 (only once it stops),
   and two quarters not yet built; until those are built the draw is among
   the five that are (Zork draws among seven, never west).
4. The Riddle Room: "What is tall as a house, round as a cup, and all the
   king's horses can't draw it up?" Say "well" and the stone door opens
   (5 points). The Pearl Room beyond holds the pearl necklace (15).
5. The Circular Room, the bottom of the well, with the wooden bucket.
   Fresh water (the teapot filled at a ford) poured into the bucket makes
   it rise; in it, the visitor rises to the Top of Well (10 points).
   Salt water from the Pool Room does nothing. The water evaporates in a
   hundred moves and the bucket goes down again; taking the water out
   sends it down at once.
6. The Tea Room: the oblong table with four cakes. The "Eat Me" cake
   makes the room huge (the visitor small): the Posts Room, where the
   table is four wooden posts and a roof and everything on it is too big
   to take, and east the Pool Room with the flask, the leak and the pool
   of tears. The red cake thrown in the pool evaporates it and shows the
   candy (15). The blue cake eaten in the Posts Room brings the visitor
   back to the Tea Room, everything restored; eaten anywhere else it
   crushes them. The orange cake explodes. The red one eaten dehydrates
   them. Read through the flask, the cakes say which is which. Cakes
   carried out of the wonderland crumble to dust.
7. The Low Room (northwest of the Tea Room), where the robot stands with
   its green paper; the Machine Room east of it, with three buttons a
   person is fried by and a robot is not; the Dingy Closet south, with
   the red crystal sphere (20). Reach for the sphere and a steel cage
   falls and gas comes in; the robot, if it is there, lifts the cage when
   told. The triangular button stops the carousel (and brings the dented
   steel box with the Stradivarius (20) up through its floor); the square
   button speeds it, which makes the Low Room electric; the round button
   slows it again. With the carousel stopped, the Low Room is where a
   visitor cannot get their bearings, and Room 8 is where the grue
   repellent is.
8. The ravine (round 3): up from the Deep Ford, the Ledge in Ravine and
   the End of Ledge, where the air is warm and smells of smoke; up a
   precarious climb from the ledge, the Tiny Room, with the oak door, its
   barred window, a keyhole under a lid, and the Dreary Room beyond it lit
   red through a crack, where the blue crystal sphere (20) sits on a dusty
   table. The rusty key is in the far keyhole: open the lid, slide the
   place mat under the door, push the letter opener into the keyhole, and
   the key falls onto the mat; take the mat and the key falls to the
   floor; unlock the door and go through. Without the mat the key is lost.
9. The dragon's country: north from the End of Ledge, the Dragon Room,
   where the huge red dragon lies across the lair's mouth, watching.
   Examine it, greet it, strike it: it grows angry and follows, out of
   mingled curiosity and anger, as far as the Stone Bridge and the Cool
   Room, where its anger cools two a move; past seven it incinerates
   whoever provoked it. Since round 4 (P6) a blow's reply is graded rather
   than drawn (a shrug, a stare, and "You had better be very careful now"
   for the blow before the last), and when its interest runs out behind a
   visitor who has walked on, they hear it turn back. Led west into the Ice Room, it meets its own
   reflection in the glacier, breathes fire, and drowns in the melt (5
   points); the dead dragon lies at the Deep Ford, the Ice Room's west
   passage opens on steam and a rockfall, heard coming down in the same
   breath as the reveal (round 4's P14), and the lair is open: the rotten
   chest with the golden statuette (20), whose hinges give on the second
   try as the first try's line promises (P2), and the princess in a trance.
10. The princess: woken (examined, spoken to, or startled by the chest's
   hinges), she walks home a step a move, glancing back: the Dragon Room,
   the ledge, down to the Deep Ford, the Marble Hall, where she presses a
   loose piece of marble and the wall slides away, the Path Near Stream,
   the garden, the gazebo. Six moves after she is home, if the visitor is
   in the gazebo or at the garden's north end, the unicorn comes to her,
   she ties her hair with the ribbon, and gives the visitor the gold key
   (15) and a rose, and rides off; if nobody is there she leaves the rose
   in the gazebo and the key is gone for good, which is Zork's.
11. The Bank of Zork: west of the Dragon Room through the Fresco Room.
   The entrance, two tellers' rooms, two viewing rooms, the Safety
   Depository whose north wall is a curtain of light, and the Chairman's
   Office with his portrait (20). The curtain opens on a room chosen by
   the way the Depository was entered: from the West Teller's room, the
   East Viewing Room; from the East Teller's, the West; from the Office,
   the Small Room, which has no exits; and from the Small Room's south
   wall back into the Depository and through the curtain again, the
   Vault, with the bills (25). For twelve moves after a pass one wall of
   the room beyond leads back; then "Curtain Door Closed," and in the
   Small Room the Gnome of Zurich comes three moves later with a deposit
   box, takes a treasure for the way out, and goes after twelve. The
   Depository's doors sound an alarm against the bills and the portrait;
   the curtain does not: carry them through it into a viewing room and
   walk out south. Since round 4 the alarm's line says it is the doorway
   that knows the Bank's property, and that the curtain was not built by
   the same people (P7).
12. The Wizard, every two minutes, one time in ten: he appears, waves his
   wand, speaks a word beginning with "F", and vanishes cackling. Feeble,
   Fumble, Fear, Filch, Freeze, Fall, Ferment, Fierce, Float, Fireproof,
   Fence, Fantasize: each changes what the visitor can do for three to
   eighteen minutes (five to thirty-five moves), and ends with its own
   line. Fence puts a fence in the room that can be examined, pushed and
   walked into. A spell is timed by the clock rather than by moves, so
   that one which refuses every move still runs out; "wait" runs the
   clock ninety seconds. Fantasize, after round 2.5, is not Zork's
   one-in-five flicker on every description: each room rolls once when
   the spell is cast, one room in two gets one of the eight, and it stays
   listed with what is really there until the spell ends.

"Done", for the two quarters, is: the riddle answered, the well risen,
the pearl, the candy, the sphere and the violin taken, the robot told
what to do and doing it, the carousel stopped, the dragon drowned, the
statuette, the gold key, the portrait, the bills and the blue sphere
taken (190 of Zork II's 400), and the Wizard met and survived. 190 is the
ceiling, and the world says so once it is reached: a line in the
narrator's voice when the last point lands, and a parenthesis on the
score line after (round 3's P7). The wand (30) waits for the Wizard's own
rooms.

## Secrets and interactions I hope players find

- **The carousel never sends you west** while it spins, so Room 8 and the
  repellent can only be found once the robot has pushed the triangular
  button. The dull thump heard in the distance is the steel box arriving.
- **The Low Room turns the tables**: once the carousel is stopped, the
  Low Room is the one that scrambles the compass, and with the square
  button pushed it kills. A player who pushes everything the robot can
  push learns the order matters.
- **The cage is a trap with a tell**: the robot, if it was in the closet,
  is caught in the cage too, bent double and humming, and the description
  says so; a player who reads it knows whom to ask. Since round 3 the cage
  has a close-up of its own (the bars nobody could lift, the gas coming
  in, the robot's absence remarked on), the gas thickens a line a move,
  and an order to the robot while it stands in another room is answered
  in voice by its stand-in, which says where it is (P2, P3).
- **Dying costs more than ten points**: the lamp goes back to the barrow,
  treasures to the Wizard's stash (out of reach until his rooms are
  built), and everything else to the gazebo; the third death is the end.
- **The sword glows** blue near the unicorn, in its room as next door
  since round 4 (P13: five runs read the bright glow as the Wizard), and
  brightly when the Wizard stands in the room and stays; it is the only
  warning of either. (It never did glow for the Wizard before round 2.5:
  a visitor is sent no `:arrived`, friction 40; he tells the sword himself
  now, and only when he lingers.)
- **The cakes crumble** carried out of the wonderland, so the flask must
  be read in the Pool Room, not in the lamp-light of the Carousel.
- **The bucket keeps the water**: climb in before pouring, or watch it go
  up without you ("The bucket swiftly rises up, and is gone."), and wait
  a hundred moves for it to come back. The tells, added after round 1
  when nobody raised it: a tide-line inside the bucket that says it has
  held water lately; the rope, taut, going up into the dark, which `look
  up` and the rope's own description point at; and, once the bucket has
  gone up alone, a line in the room that whatever hauled it up will
  presumably let it down again. After round 2, when the tells were read
  and did not help: the bucket's close-up, seen from outside, says it
  goes up with what is in it and without what is not; "wait" at the
  bottom while it is up says the wait is possible and long; "wait for
  the bucket" does it; and the rope, climbed or pulled at either end,
  answers in voice. After round 2.5 the pour from outside is warned
  before it costs: the rope goes taut and the bucket lifts at one side
  and "has not gone yet", and a visitor who climbs in on that move rises
  with it. After round 3 the wait line names the command.
- **The topiary animals** move when the visitor has stood too long among
  them, and close in, and then attack. Leave when they move. Each of
  them (the dragon, the serpent, the dog, the human figures) has a
  close-up of its own since round 2, and each carries the tell; the
  unicorn among them is hedge, and says so.
- **The Fantasize spell's things** ("There is a basilisk here.") are
  every room's own, beside the grue: reaching for one is answered in the
  narrator's voice, so a player can tell an illusion from a parser
  failure (round 2's P5). Since round 2.5 each stays put for the length
  of the spell, listed on arrival and on "look", and a room's illusion
  answers to its words only while it is shown; the copy of ZORK I answers
  to "zork" since round 3 (P4); the yellow sphere beside the closet's real
  one is a coin-toss on "take sphere", which is the Wizard's joke and not
  mine. Since round 4 the refusal opens with the verb's own clause, an eye
  for looking and a blow for striking (P11), and the grue the spell shows
  is answered for by the room's grue, which says that a grue in plain
  sight is the one thing no grue has ever been (P12).
- **Brief mode is Zork's**: a room seen again is named and no more. The
  first time it happens, one parenthetical line says LOOK, VERBOSE and
  BRIEF, and is not said again.
- **The Wizard's spells interact**: Feeble makes the load fifty; Fumble
  drops things from a full hand; Float puts the floor out of reach and,
  at the Top of Well, ends in a long fall; Fear sends the visitor fleeing
  by a random way, which in the Carousel Room is random twice over, and
  from the dragon as from the Wizard; Fierce turns the visitor on the
  dragon, which is a way to anger it; Fireproof makes the dragon's fire
  wash over harmlessly, once, and the doubling-back dragon crushes a
  fireproof visitor in its jaws instead; Filch makes anything of value
  vanish as it is touched; Fantasize puts things in rooms that are not
  there.
- **"Robot, go east"** works, comma and all, and so does "tell the robot
  to push the triangular button"; the robot answers "Whirr, buzz, click!"
  and does it, or, now and then, "Buzz! Buzz! Buzz! My circuits are
  getting rusty. Try again."
- **Taking the sphere is a trap** only if the robot is not there to lift
  the cage; a player who sends the robot south first is safe, and one who
  does not has six moves of poison gas to think it over.
- **Make a wish at the bottom of the well** and no one is listening, yet:
  the voice that tells you what makes the bucket go wants the priceless
  zorkmid, which lies on a ledge in the volcano (round 5).
- **The dragon's anger is arithmetic**: examine it for one, greet it for
  two, strike or kick it or throw at it for four, give to it for one; it
  cools by two a move; past six it kills. So two blows and a walk lose it
  by the Cool Room, and the way to the glacier is to strike it again on
  the bridge and again in the Cool Room, so that it is still interested
  when it sees itself. Sneak past it to the lair and it doubles back.
- **The dragon takes gifts**: a treasure given to it is "excused for a
  moment" and goes into the chest in its lair, where it can be had back
  once the dragon is dead; anything else is refused.
- **The princess will not be touched**: strike her and the Wizard, of all
  people, unrolls himself out of nothing and says "Fry!".
- **The princess is a clock**: she walks a step a move, three moves in
  four, and six moves after she is home the unicorn comes. A visitor who
  dawdles finds a rose in the gazebo and no key, ever. Follow her, and
  wait in the Marble Hall for the wall to open if you are ahead.
- **The Bank's brochure tells the whole trick** if read slowly: the tellers
  "seem to walk through walls". The way out with the money is through the
  curtain into a viewing room, not through a door; and the curtain opens
  on a viewing room only when the Depository was entered from a teller's
  room, so the loot must be set down, the room left and re-entered, and
  the loot picked up again.
- **The Small Room is a trap with a price**: the way in is the Office,
  and the way out, once the curtain has closed, is the south wall while it
  still shimmers, or the gnome, who takes a treasure for it. Throw the
  loot through a wall of light and it lands in the Depository; through the
  curtain, and it lands in the room the curtain opens on, and the curtain
  closes behind it.
- **The key without the mat is lost**: the push sends it somewhere that
  is not a room, and the game says nothing, as Zork says nothing. The mat
  pulled from under the door, rather than taken, is drawn out and the key
  falls off it (round 4's P9).
- **The bucket's tide-line says what water it wants**: fresh, the kind the
  stream has at a ford and a teapot carries (round 4's P4).
- **The matchbook is a light**: six matches, two moves each, for a visitor
  who has lost the lamp to the Wizard.
- **Two keys answer to "key"**: with both in hand the parser draws, and
  tells you which it drew; say "iron key" or "gold key".
